# AI Tells, Full Catalog

Reference for the `copywriter` skill. What makes writing read as AI-generated, with before/after fixes. Grounded in the Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" catalog, ChatGPT-overused-word studies (Kobak, Juzek, Merrill), and humanizing-copy guides. Sources at the bottom.

The mental model: **AI writing is too balanced, too smooth, too hedged, and too eager to sound impressive. Human writing is lumpy, plain, specific, and has a point of view that excludes other points of view.**

---

## 1. Punctuation and formatting tells

### Em dash overuse (HARD BAN)
LLMs use em dashes 2-3x the rate of human writers, usually surrounded by spaces, to fake "punched-up" sales rhythm. Even prompted to drop them, the assembled feel remains.
- ❌ `Our system saves time, real time, the kind your team feels.`
- ✅ `Our system saves time. The kind your team actually feels.`
- Fix menu: period, comma, colon, parentheses, or restructure. Never " - " as a stand-in.

### Emoji (HARD BAN)
Section breaks with emoji, ✅/🔥/🚀 bullets, a rocket after a claim. Instant "content marketing bot" read.
- Fix: delete. Let the sentence carry it.

### Boldface spray
Bolding every key term like a slide deck or readme ("key takeaways" reflex).
- ❌ `A **workshop** for **business owners** who want **real AI systems**.`
- ✅ `A workshop for business owners who want real AI systems.`
- Fix: bold at most one thing in short copy, usually nothing.

### Title Case Headings And Symmetric Lists
Every heading Capitalized The Same Way, every bullet the same shape and length.
- Fix: sentence case, uneven bullets.

## 2. Structure and rhythm tells

### Rule of three (tricolon)
The single most common structural tell. "adjective, adjective, adjective" or "phrase, phrase, and phrase", used to make thin points look thorough.
- ❌ `Faster, smarter, and more reliable workflows.`
- ✅ `Workflows that don't break at 2pm.`
- Fix: use one strong item, or two, or four. Break the triple.

### Contrastive scaffolding
"It's not just X, it's Y." / "not only... but also." / "This isn't about A. It's about B."
- ❌ `This isn't just training. It's a transformation.`
- ✅ `You leave with one working system your team uses Monday.`
- Fix: cut the setup, state Y directly. If you can't say Y plainly, you don't have Y.

### Perfect balance / uniform sentence length
Every sentence medium-length, evenly weighted, metronomic.
- Fix: vary hard. Long sentence that carries a full thought, then a short one. Three words. Real prose is lumpy.

### Over-explaining and runway
Setup before the point, defining what the reader knows, restating for "clarity".
- ❌ `In order to help you understand the value, it's worth noting that AI, when applied correctly, can...`
- ✅ `AI cuts your ops budget. Here's the one process we'll fix first.`
- Fix: cut the runway, land the plane.

## 3. Word-choice tells

### AI vocabulary (overused words)
One or two is coincidence. A cluster is the strongest single tell.
- **Verbs:** delve, leverage, utilize, harness, streamline, underscore, foster, elevate, unlock, empower, facilitate, enable, showcase, boast, tailor.
- **Adjectives:** crucial, pivotal, robust, seamless, vibrant, rich, profound, comprehensive, multifaceted, innovative, cutting-edge, bespoke, myriad.
- **Nouns:** landscape, realm, tapestry, synergy, testament, journey, ecosystem, cornerstone, game-changer, treasure trove, world of.
- Fix: plain synonym or delete. use, important, has, is, helps.

### Avoiding plain "is/are/has"
AI replaces copulas with marketing verbs: `serves as`, `stands as`, `boasts`, `features`, `offers`, `represents`.
- ❌ `The venue serves as the location and offers a warm setting.`
- ✅ `The venue is warm and quiet.`

### Inflated transitions / conjunctive adverbs
Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Consequently, Notably, Importantly, "It is important to note that".
- Fix: delete, or use "and", "also", "but", or just a new sentence.

### Isolated wordy constructions
`in order to` (→ to), `as a result of` (→ because), `a wide range of` (→ name them), `the fact that` (→ that), `at this point in time` (→ now).

## 4. Tone and content tells

### Promotional puffery
Travel-brochure register even when neutral is wanted: "nestled in the heart of", "natural beauty", "renowned for", "rich cultural heritage", "commitment to excellence".
- Fix: state facts. Cut adjectives that praise rather than describe.

### Vague attribution / weasel words
"Many experts believe", "studies show", "industry observers note", "it's widely regarded", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Fix: name the source, or cut the claim. Specificity beats authority-borrowing.

### Over-hedging
"perhaps", "arguably", "it could be said", "tends to", "may potentially". AI hedges to avoid being wrong.
- Fix: commit. Have a point of view. If you're unsure, say what you do know.

### Empty conclusions
"In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall", "At the end of the day" followed by a restatement.
- Fix: end on the actual last point. Short copy needs no wrap-up.

### No point of view
AI describes all sides evenly and reveals nothing about the writer. Human writing is selective: it excludes, it has opinions, it says what surprised the writer or what they got wrong.
- Fix: take a side. Cut what you don't believe. One honest specific ("your team wastes 6 hours a week re-typing orders") beats ten balanced generalities.

## The strip-pass checklist

Read the draft once for each:
- [ ] Zero `—` / `–` / stand-in " - "
- [ ] Zero emoji
- [ ] No rule-of-three stacks (max one, and only if earned)
- [ ] No "not just X, it's Y" scaffolding
- [ ] No AI-vocab cluster (delve/leverage/robust/seamless/tapestry/etc.)
- [ ] Plain is/are/has, not serves-as/boasts/features
- [ ] No Furthermore/Moreover/Notably openers
- [ ] No vague "experts say" attribution
- [ ] No "In conclusion/Overall" wrap-up
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary (read aloud)
- [ ] At least one concrete specific, not all generalities
- [ ] It has a point of view
